Domain

The research behind the storybooks

What the literature says, where it falls short, and how this project addresses it.

Literature Survey

A review of IEEE Xplore, Scopus, ScienceDirect and Google Scholar covering 2012–2025.

AR in primary education

Augmented reality consistently improves engagement and conceptual understanding in primary STEM, especially for content that is spatial, dynamic or invisible, such as forces, life cycles and measurement.

Narrative learning

AR storybooks such as Metabook show that a continuous story sustains young learners' attention and gives each activity a purpose, but existing titles are generic and not curriculum-aligned.

Sri Lankan context

Sri Lanka has strong access to basic education but is at an emerging stage of classroom technology. Instruction relies on printed textbooks, and manipulatives are unevenly distributed.

Research Gap

No reviewed system combines all of the properties a Sri Lankan primary classroom needs.

Monolingual

AR learning tools are overwhelmingly English-only. None support Sinhala-medium learners.

Single subject

Each tool covers one subject, so every subject brings a new app and a new interaction model.

Connectivity

Offline operation is rarely reported, yet many rural schools have unreliable internet.

Curriculum fit

Systems are built for curricula outside South Asia and do not map to the Sri Lankan Grade 4–5 syllabus.

The gap: a bilingual, offline, multi-domain AR framework, built around curriculum-aligned narrative storybooks and evaluated in a real Sri Lankan classroom.

Research Problem

How can a narrative-based, bilingual AR storybook framework help Sri Lankan Grade 4–5 students understand STEAM concepts that static textbook illustrations cannot show?

The Challenge

Grade 4–5 students struggle with topics that need visual and process-based reasoning, from measurement instruments and life cycles to circuits, fractions and place value.

The Context

These concepts unfold in three dimensions and over time. Reduced to a printed illustration, learners have no way to observe the process itself.

Research Objectives

Main objective

Design, build and evaluate a bilingual, offline, narrative-based AR storybook framework for Sri Lankan Grade 4–5 STEAM education.

Specific objectives

  • Develop the Basic Measurement AR storybook, "A Measuring Day at Grandma's".
  • Develop the Basic Life Science AR storybook, "Wonders of the Living World".
  • Develop the Basic Physical Science AR storybook, "Dasun and the Moonstone".
  • Develop the Basic Number Concepts AR storybook, "Dinal and the Prism Temple".
  • Provide one shared Sinhala–English content layer and tap-based interaction model across all four components.
  • Run fully offline on ordinary Android phones, with optional anonymised log sync.
  • Evaluate learning gains, usability and engagement in a real classroom.

Research Questions

  • RQ1 · Learning outcome

    What learning gains follow narrative-based AR storybook instruction, and is post-instruction performance higher than after textbook instruction?

  • RQ2 · Usability & engagement

    How do learners and teachers experience usability, engagement and feedback quality, and what do the interaction logs show about on-task behaviour?

Methodology

Four architecturally independent but structurally consistent apps: the same offline Vuforia + Unity pipeline and the same bilingual services layer.

AR content pipeline

  1. Printed Bilingual Storybook

    Each page is both a narrative panel and a Vuforia ImageTarget, authored in Adobe Illustrator.

  2. Vuforia Marker Recognition

    The phone camera recognises the page and reports its identifier.

  3. Unity AR Content Engine

    Anchors the matching Blender-modelled 3D scene to the plane of the page.

  4. Four Curriculum Components

    Measurement · Life Science · Physical Science · Number Concepts.

Shared services layer

Bilingual Content Layer

Sinhala and English labels, instructions and audio narration, toggleable by learner or teacher.

Interaction Manager

One consistent tap-based model reused across all four components.

Learning Feedback System

Animated responses, hints and audio confirmation after every interaction.

Learning Evaluation Module

Logs AR time, quiz time, on-task time, mistakes and retries per session.

Evaluation design

A quasi-experimental, non-equivalent control-group study at a private tuition institute in Sri Lanka in August 2026. Students completed a 12-item, 55-point curriculum-aligned test written in Sinhala and reviewed by teachers. Engagement was measured through instrumented AR sessions, a student survey and a teacher panel.

Reading these results

Assignment was by intact class and the control group sat no pre-test, so the study shows an association between AR instruction and higher attainment rather than firmly attributed causation. The single-session, immediate post-test design means the effect is an upper bound.

What worked

Teachers rated attention retention and narrative enjoyment highest (4.8/5). Favourite activities were spread evenly across all four domains, so no single component carried the appeal.

What to improve

6 students (18.8%) found instructions hard to follow, and 14 reported at least one difficulty. Audio clarity, 3D manipulation and curriculum-level calibration are the main refinement targets.

Next steps

A randomised multi-site trial with a pre-tested control and delayed post-test, device-level benchmarking, and item-level response capture for instrument validation.

Technologies Used

Unity 3D

Cross-platform engine running the AR scenes, interactions and C# app logic.

Vuforia Engine

On-device ImageTarget recognition and tracking of the printed storybook pages.

Blender

Modelling and animation of the 3D objects, characters and processes.

Adobe Illustrator

Storybook illustration and layout, designed to double as AR markers.

Firebase

Optional sync of anonymised session logs when a connection is available.

Android

Target platform: ordinary Android 8.0+ phones, no headsets or depth sensors.

Fully offline

Tracking and rendering run on-device after install.

Low-cost hardware

No headsets, depth sensors or peripherals.

Privacy by design

Logs keyed to anonymous session and device IDs only.
